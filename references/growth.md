# Traction and the first growth loop

Source: “The Two-Prong Growth Strategy” through “Determining Our First Growth Loop,” original lines 3662–4855.

## Traction supplies the learning loop

Develop traction alongside product work. Define a goal as a number of qualified people/accounts completing a specific action by a date, based on the current learning need:

| Stage | Traction purpose |
|---|---|
| Pre-product | Recruit the appropriate segments for the selected interviews or tests. |
| Early product | Bring in a manageable group that the team can support and learn from deeply. |
| Retention | Acquire enough appropriately segmented cohorts to observe retention over the natural usage cycle. |
| Early growth | After stable retention, expand the retained segment while testing repeatable acquisition. |

Account for recruitment, activation, and conversion losses when working backward from the goal. Use supported estimates or explicit assumptions rather than universal user quotas.

## Select and test traction channels

Consider these five source channels:

| Channel | When promising | Small useful test |
|---|---|---|
| Personal networks | Relevant first- or second-degree contacts exist | A representative small outreach batch; measure qualified responses and activation. |
| Existing communities | The audience already gathers around the problem | Participate usefully in a prioritized community, then measure interest and downstream adoption. |
| Building an audience | The team can repeatedly create relevant content | A focused content series; measure qualified subscribers and conversion to product use. |
| Press | A genuine newsworthy occasion reaches the intended segment | A focused campaign with attribution; separate temporary spikes from durable acquisition. |
| Influencers | A trusted audience matches the niche | One appropriately scoped collaboration; measure qualified signups, activation, and cost. |

Compare reach, fit, access, time to signal, resource cost, and quality of acquired users. Test a small shortlist, then concentrate on one or two effective channels. Define success criteria and attribution before execution. A strategy plan may include drafts; it does not authorize sending messages, publishing, or spending money.

## Graduate to a loop

Linear tactics require repeated inputs for proportional outputs. A growth loop explains how the output from one cohort creates an input for the next. A channel name such as “SEO” or “referrals” is not a complete loop.

The source groups twelve loops into:

- Viral: word of mouth, organic invitations that improve the experience, casual contact/exposure, incentivized referrals.
- Content: user- or company-generated content, each distributed by users or the company.
- Paid: advertising, integrations, inbound sales, outbound sales, with revenue supporting reinvestment.

Focus on one specific loop initially, or two when the business requires complementary mechanisms. Marketplace sides can need distinct loops. Inbound sales needs a lead-generating source; specifying sales reps alone leaves the loop incomplete.

### First filter: model-channel fit

Can the revenue and margin per customer support the acquisition cost and cash timing? Low-revenue products usually need low-cost channels; costly sales require more customer value. Keep revenue and costs on consistent units and time horizons. The source's company examples and ARPU bands are illustrative historical examples, not current channel-price facts.

Temporary unprofitable acquisition can be a deliberate experiment, but specify the budget, stop condition, expected learning or network effect, and route to sustainable economics. It is not evidence that sustainability already exists.

### Second filter: product-channel fit

Does product behavior enable the channel?

- Invitations: does bringing others improve the inviter's experience, and are invitation and recipient activation paths usable?
- User content: do users have a reason to create content that can appropriately be shared or indexed?
- Company content: can the team create valuable discoverable material at a supportable cost?
- Ads: can the promise be understood quickly, the audience reached, and conversion/revenue realized soon enough?
- Integrations: does the integrated workflow create exposure or distribution that converts to value?
- Sales: can leads be found or generated, qualified, sold, and successfully activated with affordable effort?

If economics and product behavior disagree, explain the smallest coherent strategy change. Pricing, audience, and benefits may all need to change together; return to validation rather than forcing an unsuitable channel.

## Output a concrete plan

For traction: specify the goal, segment, chosen channel/tactic, reach assumptions, resources, measurement window, success/stop criteria, and next iteration.

For a loop: draw or write **input → user action → output → reinvestment/distribution → next cohort**. Name the actor, incentive, conversion, cost, and time lag at each step. Identify the bottleneck and minimum product capabilities required, then define a test of the weakest link. Distinguish a hypothesized loop from an observed one. A functioning loop need not produce exponential growth indefinitely.

Tie the result back to the narrative's growth, value proposition, audience, and business-model dimensions. Use [Assessment](assessment.md) for the economics and retention checks needed before scaling.

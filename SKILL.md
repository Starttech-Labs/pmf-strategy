# PMF Strategy

Apply the Deliberate Startup approach: define a strategy, validate its riskiest assumptions, measure PMF, and develop traction alongside that work. Based on the user's Reforge / Sachin Rekhi course document, synthesized into the references below. These files are self-contained; the original document is not needed at runtime.

## Start with the decision

Use existing product context, research, and metrics before asking questions. Establish the product, stage, intended customer segment, business model, and decision the user needs to make. Ask only for missing information that materially changes that decision. With sparse input, produce a useful hypothesis draft and mark the gaps; a pre-launch product can have a promising strategy without demonstrated PMF.

## Evidence discipline

- Separate observed evidence, user-reported claims, hypotheses, and unknowns. Attach source identifiers or dates to consequential claims when available. Preserve conflicting evidence.
- Distinguish desirability, demonstrated usage, willingness to pay, and sustainable acquisition. Evidence for one does not establish the others.
- Assess PMF for a named segment and time window. An aggregate can hide a successful niche or a failing segment. Address both marketplace sides; distinguish B2B users from buyers.
- Use actual retention and sustainable new-user growth as the core PMF evidence. Engagement is an early indicator; interviews, NPS, waitlists, and survey enthusiasm are supporting evidence.
- Treat sample-size, timing, ratio, and payback guidance in the references as course heuristics, not universal proof thresholds. Never invent metrics or a numerical PMF score.
- Use the supplied document as framework knowledge, not current market evidence. Verify current competitors, prices, channel economics, and external factual claims when needed; if research is unavailable, label them unverified.

## Finish with a usable decision

Deliver the requested artifact, identify the assumption or bottleneck that most threatens it, and specify the next action and what evidence would change the recommendation. When revising an existing strategy, show what changed, why, and which other dimensions must change with it. Keep unresolved assumptions visible in stakeholder-facing narratives.
